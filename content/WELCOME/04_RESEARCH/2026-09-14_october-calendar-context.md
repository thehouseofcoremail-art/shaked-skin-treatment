## 1. Israeli calendar — Sukkot / Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah 2026

- **Source:** Hebcal ("Sukkot 2026 – Feast of Booths," hebcal.com/holidays/sukkot-2026);
  cross-checked against Chabad.org's 2026 Jewish holiday list.
- **URL:** https://www.hebcal.com/holidays/sukkot-2026
- **Date published:** ongoing/reference calendar (not a single publish date; treated as
  a standing reference).
- **Date observed:** 2026-09-14.
- **Classification:** `fact`.
- **Finding:** Sukkot 2026 runs sundown 2026-09-25 to nightfall 2026-10-02. In Israel,
  Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah are the same combined day: sunset 2026-10-02 to
  nightfall 2026-10-03. So the chag tail lands on **October 1–3, 2026** (Thu–Sat), and
  **Sunday October 4 is the first ordinary day back** — school, work and normal scrolling
  resume.
- **Relevance / calendar decision:** October's calendar treats Oct 1–3 as a deliberate
  **content pause** (no feed posts, no stories) out of the same good taste a brand
  observes around any major chag, not as a guess. Sunday Oct 4 becomes the real, dateable
  hook for the month's opening post ("back to real life" / group-chat pileup) — a
  calendar moment with a verified date, not an invented one.

## 2. Israeli wedding-season timing — treated as unresolved, not asserted

- **Source:** general bridal-planning search (Reform Judaism, Smashing the Glass,
  18Doors — all US/Diaspora-oriented Jewish wedding planning sites), plus general
  industry chatter that October is a busy post-chagim booking window in Israel
  specifically.
- **URL:** https://reformjudaism.org/5-things-consider-when-choosing-your-wedding-date ;
  https://www.smashingtheglass.com/wedding-planning-timeline-checklist/
- **Date observed:** 2026-09-14.
- **Classification:** `trend signal` — **conflicting, not verified**. The sources found
  frame October as a month couples *avoid* around Sukkot itself, which is true for the
  chag days but says nothing about the well-known Israeli-market habit of booking
  weddings in the weeks right after the chagim end. I could not find a dated, sourced
  statistic for "October is Israel's second wedding peak" — it may be true, it may be
  vendor folklore.
- **Decision:** the calendar does **not** state or imply any claim about October being
  Israel's busiest wedding month. It only uses the *verified date* of the chagim ending
  (fact #1 above) as the seasonal hook, phrased as "life picks back up," never as a
  volume or popularity claim. Flagged to Romy in blocking questions in case she has real
  local booking data worth using next month.

## 3. 2026 Instagram/TikTok wedding content formats

- **Source:** TikTok discover pages ("2026 Wedding Trend," "#2026weddingtrends") and
  Nice News, "2026 Wedding Trends Spotlight Gen Z and TikTok."
- **URL:** https://nicenews.com/culture/2026-wedding-trends/ ;
  https://www.tiktok.com/discover/2026-wedding-trend
- **Date observed:** 2026-09-14.
- **Classification:** `trend signal`.
- **Finding:** (1) POV-format planning content is dominant — screen-recording-style or
  text-overlay "POV: it's wedding week" beats, not staged photography. (2) Couples and
  content are moving away from generic/traditional details toward personality-driven,
  guest-experience-forward moments — interactive seating/arrival experiences get called
  out specifically as a 2026 focus. (3) Aesthetic direction: organic/vintage florals,
  warm earthy palettes, individuality over maximalism.
- **Bridge (per the trend-adaptation rule):**
  **Trend** — POV/"nobody tells you" format dramatizing planning friction →
  **Friction it exposes** — guest logistics and communication is the unglamorous, most
  draining part of planning, and it's the part couples don't see coming →
  **Welcome feature that solves it** — **UNVERIFIED**, no confirmed feature list exists
  yet (see open blocker below), so this month's Reels use the format to dramatize the
  friction and build brand affinity only; they stop short of naming a solution →
  **Metric it moves** — engagement (comments/shares) and awareness, not conversion.
  This is exactly the discipline the operating rules require: format adapted, product
  claim withheld until verified.
- **Guest-experience note used directly:** the "what guests actually remember" feed
  post (Oct 25) is built on this signal — it dramatizes the experience side of a wedding
  without attaching an unverified feature to it.
